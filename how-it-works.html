How it works

Our process, in plain language

You should know exactly what happens after you give. Here's how a lab goes from idea to students logging on.

How we choose schools

We look for rural schools and learning centers with no computer access or internet, a committed team of teachers, and a trusted local partner. Many come to us through Asha for Education's volunteer network and our existing partners. [Confirm/expand on your criteria.]

How we verify needs

Before we commit, we talk directly with school leaders and our on-the-ground partner to understand how many students the lab will serve, what space and power are available, and whether internet service can reach the school. [Add: site visits, photos, or written requests, if applicable.]

How equipment is purchased and installed

We purchase fully functional desktop computers or laptops and wifi routers, [locally in-country / in the U.S. — confirm], so equipment is compatible and easy to service. Our team and partners then set up the complete lab: machines, networking, and core software like Chrome and Google Workspace.

How we confirm labs are working

A lab only counts once students are actually using it. We confirm every machine is online and collect photos, videos, and feedback from teachers. Where possible, our team is there in person. In Kathmandu, we spent about two weeks on site.

How we follow up

We stay in touch with each partner school, check in on how the lab is being used, help with curriculum (like Khan Academy and AI fundamentals), and sometimes keep teaching remotely over Zoom. [Add your check-in schedule, e.g. "every 3 months".]
